Best way to build a crystal

  • Red outlines mark the cost frontier: every tile that beats all tiles costing the same or less. By default every stack costs 1, so this is the best tile for each total stack count (bonus + cascade). The red band smooths them.
  • Crystal cost (above the grid) sets your own prices. A tile costs bonus cost × bonus stacks above your free bonus + cascade cost × cascade stacks above your free cascade. Free stacks cost nothing but count toward the tile, and tiles below them are left out. A budget outlines in gold the best tile you can afford and opens its details. The posters show the default costs.
  • Up to about 60 total stacks the best split is roughly even: 15/15 at 30 stacks, 27/18 at 45, 33/27 at 60.
  • Beyond that, extra stacks pay more as bonus than as cascade: 54/36 at 90, 75/45 at 120, 84/66 at 150. Cascade fills its ±3 cube and stops adding chests, while bonus places new sources.
  • The chart stops at bonus 90 and has no bonus 61–90 cells past cascade 90, so above ~150 total stacks the frontier can only add cascade and sits at 87/90.

Which ability wins where

  • The low corner is chain's. With bonus and cascade both under 30, chain leads in 79 of 100 cells (9 ties, 12 vein). With bonus 30+ and cascade under 30, chain leads in 150 of 210.
  • Cascade hands it to vein. With bonus under 30 and cascade 30+, vein leads in 632 of 710 cells. From cascade 93 up, vein leads in all 1,050.
  • Where vein takes over (first cascade from which vein stays ≥ 5 % ahead): bonus 3 → cascade 60, 6 → 63, 9 → 66, 12 → 60, 15 → 60, 18 → 51, 21 → 54, 24 → 51, 30 → 45, 36 → 39, 45 → 33, 51 → 27, 60 → 24, 72 → 21, 90 → 15. Bonus 0 rooms hold too few chests to call.
  • Clumpiness sorts it. Below clumpiness 20 chain wins 195 of 304 cells. At 20–40 it depends on the mix (vein 155, tie 47, chain 63). From 40 up chain never wins; vein takes 249 of 270 at 40–60 and every cell from 60.

Cascade stops adding chests

  • Cascade copies a chest into free floor spots in the ±3 cube around it, and copies never cascade again. Once those spots are full, extra stacks do little.
  • Chests per room reach 95 % of their cascade-240 level by cascade 102–144 at every bonus from 6 up.
  • Best cpm from cascade 120 to the cascade 150–240 average: bonus 30 3,541 → 3,769, bonus 45 5,242 → 5,495, bonus 60 7,515 → 7,482.

What changed from v2.1

  • The room picker is priced properly. v2.1 drew rooms at random with the picker's alignment frequencies, which kept the picker's sparse rooms without the choice that made them worth taking. A vault-level simulation of the in-game picker (depth-4 lookahead, planned door neighbours, the miner's own proxy) on shape-priced 24 × 24 vault grids beat a straight run along the dense axis in all 16 cell-miner cases: chain +5.3 % (+1.7 to +8.5), vein +7.0 % (+1.0 to +14.0), with no trend in clumpiness. Against a random lane it was +11–19 % chain and +19–35 % vein.
  • So each cell is now 64 shared rooms along a straight run at x-alignment 0 (z cycling, straight through each room), and chain rates are ×1.053 and vein ×1.070 for the picker. Best cpm is a median ×1.10 of v2.1 (p10 ×1.05, p90 ×1.17), and vein/chain moved ×1.02 further toward vein.
  • The dashed range outlines are gone. Rooms from about 300 to 1,800 chests are where the time model was fitted; the dense top rows and the bonus 0–9 rows sit outside that.
  • Chart numbers apply to living, gilded and ornate alike: the three share one modifier definition (8 bonus attempts per chunk, 0.25 cascade per stack), and generating the same cells as each type gives the same chests (±1 %) and clumpiness (±3 %). Count a map's "+1 %" cascade stacks as 1/25 of a stack each.
  • Same method otherwise: 64 shared rooms per cell (chest counts never fall with more stacks across bonus and cascade 0–90; best cpm dips in 6 % of +3 steps, 2 by more than 5 %), 733 cells planned both ways, and on the lattice a neighbour-based prediction missed by 4 % with no wrong winner among 153 clear calls.

Why vein gained against v1

  • Measured on 36 cells by swapping one ingredient at a time from the v1 setup to the v2 one (×1.19 in vein/chain overall): new rooms ×1.05; the shape model's movement pricing ×0.98; bail 0.6 and pruning as in game ×1.03; the 5 s room switch replaced by the measured ~1 s ×1.07 (vein rooms are shorter, so a flat 5 s hurt vein more); vein's own fitted room overhead, switch and collected share ×1.09; vein's own click costs ×0.965.
  • The weakest piece is the room overhead: the fit gives chain 2.56 s per room of entry and exit against vein's 1.75 s, with no known mechanism, and chain and vein were logged on different vaults. The collected share (0.900 chain, 0.924 vein) is measured directly.

How close to real play

  • On 12 logged vaults, this projection method (planned chests × collected share over shape-model time plus switches) lands within about 2–3 % of the achieved rate, vault by vault.
  • Your straight-run laps on living crystals, rerun at your speed (0.283) and at the chunk alignment those laps actually had, time-weighted real vs chart: 45/45 chain −11 % learned / −6 % freehand; 30/30 chain −1 % / +5 %; 21/56 chain +2 % learned / +7 % simple; 51/74 vein −7 % learned / −9 % simple / −17 % freehand.
  • Your gilded 35/71 vein vault (picker on): the straight-run rooms at its speed give 3,089–3,188 cpm and ×1.07 for the picker 3,305–3,411, against 3,044 (learned lap) and 3,191 (shape lap) real over room and switch time. The picker's gain did not fully show there (the straight-ahead room was unloaded at 69 % of its decisions), so read the chart's picker uplift as an upper end until more picker laps are logged.
  • Not modelled: special rooms, mobs, warps beyond their average cost, reconnects.

Clumpiness is the size of the touching-chest group the average chest sits in (Σ size² ÷ chests), counting diagonal and corner contact the way the vein miner does.
